# Methodology

## Approach
Desk research using free, public tools only: web search, publisher pages, Lindt press releases and reports, US Department of Labor and NORC publications, and docket aggregators. Each fact was recorded with its URL and the date accessed, and labelled with a confidence level.

## Confidence levels
- **Confirmed**: stated in a primary or official source (e.g. NORC/DOL findings, Lindt's own release).
- **Reported**: stated by credible press or secondary sources, not verified against a primary document.
- **Alleged**: a claim made in the lawsuit complaint. Unproven; Lindt denies it.
- **Unverified**: inferred or illustrative, with no direct source.

## Confirmed vs. inferred
- The network's brand → factory → supplier structure is partly **inferred**. No Lindt suppliers are named; processor and supplier nodes are generic placeholders, and factory-level supply edges are Unverified.
- Map flow thickness is an **illustrative** weight, not trade volume.
- Cocoa-region polygons are hand-drawn and approximate. Regional child-labor prevalence is not available, so shading is sector-level.
- The case number 1:26-cv-02569 was read from a docket URL in press coverage and should be confirmed on CourtListener.
- US factory locations come from secondary reporting and should be confirmed against Lindt's annual report.

## Limitations and open gaps
- Year of Lindt's "8,491 visits / 87 child workers" figure is unresolved (2021 per one outlet; another summary says 2023). Original report not yet cited directly.
- Whether Lindt met its 2025 monitoring-coverage goal is unconfirmed.
- DOL list-of-goods entries for Ghana not verified; DOL and docket pages blocked automated access.
- Pending: USITC DataWeb imports, ImportYeti shipments, OpenSanctions screening, NORC country split.
